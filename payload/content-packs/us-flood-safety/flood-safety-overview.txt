FIELDOS-EDITED FLOOD SAFETY OVERVIEW

This is an edited FieldOS summary of National Weather Service (NWS) material. It is not an official NWS publication, does not provide current forecasts or warnings, and is not a site-specific flood plan. Check current local official instructions before you lose connectivity. If an emergency is happening, follow emergency officials.

PLAN BEFORE FLOODING

- Find out whether your home, work, school, and usual roads are in flood-prone areas. Identify a route to higher ground before an event; do not assume this offline map shows flood zones or safe routes.
- Make a household communication and meeting plan. Include pets and anyone who may need help evacuating.
- Keep essentials ready, including drinking water, food that needs little or no cooking, needed medicines, a battery-powered radio, batteries, lights, and basic first-aid supplies. This is a planning reminder, not a complete supply list.
- Heavy rain, upstream rainfall, blocked waterways, or infrastructure failure can cause flooding. Flash flooding may develop rapidly, including away from where heavy rain fell.

WHEN WATER IS RISING

- Move to higher ground and leave early when flooding is likely or officials direct you to evacuate. Do not wait for a road to become impassable.
- Never walk, swim, or drive through floodwater or past a barricade. Turn back and use a different route. Water can hide washed-out roads, debris, electrical hazards, and contamination; its depth and current may be hard to judge.
- Keep away from streams, storm drains, culverts, and low crossings. Keep children out of floodwater and drainage areas.
- Follow evacuation instructions. If trapped by moving water, move to the highest safe point available and contact emergency services if possible.
- Do not enter a room where water covers electrical outlets or submerged cords. If you see sparks or hear electrical buzzing, crackling, or popping, leave the area and stay out of the water.

AFTER FLOODING

Follow current instructions from local officials before returning or using roads, buildings, utilities, or water supplies. This static reference cannot tell you whether a location is safe now.
